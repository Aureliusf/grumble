Distress doesn't show up as overeating. It shows up as skipped meals, no fiber, and a glucose number drifting up.

NHANES 2011–2014, 808 US cancer survivors, by PHQ-9 depression score: none (0–4, n=493) vs severe (15+, n=42). Unweighted medians, observational. There's no loneliness question in NHANES; Grumble asks it.

  • Every day Grumble asks "How's the day going?", not "rate your mood".
  • Three low days in a week and the coach calls. Quietly, no alarm on screen.
  • It never labels anyone: no "lonely", no "depressed", no "poor".
  • It never plays friend. It gets you to a person: the table, a walking buddy, the coach.
~30 s. "This is real data: 808 cancer survivors in NHANES. The ones with severe depressive symptoms eat 370 fewer calories a day and a third less fiber, and twice as many have diabetes. Distress shows up as skipped meals, not binges. So Grumble asks how the day is going, sends the coach after three low days, and never labels anyone. And it never pretends to be your friend: it gets you to a person."

What we built

One loop a day, built around the food you already eat.

Intake

Learns as you go

Starts with one meal you love, in your words. Every visit adds to it; nothing you said no to comes back.

Today

Eat, then balance

Log a burger at lunch and dinner becomes lean protein and vegetables. A spike bar and a running "how today's going".

What if

Your dish, your clock

Same plate at 7 vs 9:30, with or without a walk, on a curve: what happened to people like you, never a forecast.

The table

Tuesdays at Versailles

Survivors host. Donors fund a seat for about $20. The menu has the portions worked out. Nobody pays.

The path · ~2½ min

  1. Today: the nudge. "Dinner was at 9:40 last night. Not a crime."
  2. Log lunch in plain words. "a burger and fries"
  3. Feedback + spike bar. Dinner turns lean; the burger stays.
  4. Check in → the table card. "What is this?" Nobody pays.
  5. What if → whole day. Balanced vs usual.
  6. Table → seat saved. Fund a week.
